Plan B: Session management first

Fieldbook's highest-value purpose is a reliable GM desk with a controlled player companion, not a world simulator. Complete existing session workflows before expanding interconnected simulation systems.

Alternative proposal, not an adopted roadmap. These priorities are recommendations based on the feature assessment. They do not remove existing requirements or change implementation status.

Priority order

Eight priorities, from essential campaign reliability to optional simulation.
PriorityFeaturesAssessment
1 - EssentialSetup-to-runtime handoff, persistence, recovery, authorization, player-data isolation.Fix the documented imported-campaign dashboard initialization gap first. Everything depends on trustworthy state.
2 - Core session toolsCharacters, inventory, conditions, journal, campaign clock, personnel.Support common session tasks with fast, complete manual workflows before adding automated mechanics.
3 - Shared awarenessMap, locations, route, party position, explicit report and map publication.Make shared information reliable and keep private campaign data behind deliberate GM publication.
4 - Rules assistanceCombat calculators, weather, travel, ammunition and supply bookkeeping.Favor source-checked correctness and usable integration over increasing the number of mechanics.
5 - Operational reliabilityScheduled backups, portable map assets, actionable errors, conflict handling.Complete before relying on long campaigns. Real PMTiles rendering and recovery still need verification.
6 - Session preparationNPC and character creation, missions, encounters, opposing forces, settlement drafts.Useful time-savers, but editable templates and manual entry can deliver much of the initial value.
7 - Campaign depthFaction relationships, settlement needs, threats, territorial annotations.Start with simple records and explicit GM-entered changes; expand for demonstrated campaign needs.
8 - Advanced simulationEconomy, production, workforce, diplomacy automation, persistent world simulation.Highest complexity and least universal benefit. Defer until actual play demonstrates demand.

Candidates to remove, reduce, or defer

  • Full economic simulation: Workforce, prosperity, production, dynamic pricing, and supply/demand can become a separate strategy game. Settlement needs, available goods, and GM-set prices may be enough.
  • Autonomous or AI-driven diplomacy: Use relationship notes and GM-authored events rather than autonomous political outcomes.
  • Integrated world simulation: Prefer a dated event queue with GM approval to scheduling every economic, political, and military interaction.
  • Separate dashboards for every subsystem: Start with contextual panels over shared records instead of major logistics, market, diplomacy, conflict, territory, and intelligence surfaces.
  • Advanced messaging: Defer read receipts, inbox deletion, and push delivery; dependable shared COMMS with polling may suffice for LAN play.
  • Advanced mapping: Defer vector styling, fog masking, geometry history, and elaborate editing. Reliable markers, routes, and publication cover the primary need.
  • AI-assisted setup: Keep it optional and outside the critical path. Manual campaign authoring must remain fully usable.
  • Broad battle simulation: Keep it separate from existing attack calculators and build only if campaign play requires it.

Documentation and evidence

Consolidate duplicated combat and status descriptions that disagree about planned, implemented, and production-ready behavior. Implemented calculations alone do not establish complete rules coverage or production readiness.

Recommended decision: Finish the session-management product before expanding the simulation product. Retain manual factions, settlements, resources, and events; postpone their automated interactions.

Reference proposal only. No campaign behavior, feature commitments, or official rules are changed by this page.
